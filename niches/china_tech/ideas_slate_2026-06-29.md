controls; Chinese domestic resist real but lagging at the leading edge. Hedge all shares.
Named giant: JSR / Shin-Etsu / Tokyo Ohka. Protagonist: one wafer, from coat to develop.

## jade_protocol — ONE IRRATIONAL-LOOKING QUESTION × 3 + reframe (LOCK reading; first-person investigator)

### Idea A — TITLE: Why Is Apple Begging Washington to Buy Chips From a Blacklisted Company?

Driving question (carries the title): why would the world's most demanding, most cautious company —
Apple — lobby Washington for permission to buy memory from a Pentagon-listed supplier (CXMT), risking
its reputation for a chip still behind the leaders?
Three answers, escalating: (1) Popular: it's the shortage, Apple just needs volume. Incomplete — Apple
avoided risky sources for years. (2) Deeper: leverage against Samsung / SK Hynix / Micron. Incomplete —
explains pressure, not the political risk. (3) Real mechanism: when memory becomes a strategic shortage,
supply security outweighs politics — Apple is buying a second valve nobody can shut, not cheapness.
LOCK reading: the barrier was meant to keep CXMT off the market; read backward, Apple even asking proves
the shortage made Chinese memory too valuable to ignore.
Honest turn: bullish — validation by the strictest buyer. Bearish — Washington can block it; the deal
may never sign. Synthesis: the request itself matters, not the deal.
Reframe close: the question was never "will Apple buy," it is "what happened to memory if even Apple is
rewriting who counts as an acceptable supplier."
Anchor (verify): Apple lobbying for clearance to buy DRAM from CXMT; CXMT = 4th-largest DRAM, on the
Pentagon list; memory shortage acute in 2026. Do NOT quote prices verbatim (OCR contradictions in source).
Named giant: Apple + Samsung / SK Hynix / Micron. Protagonist: one memory module from a Hefei fab to a MacBook.

### Idea B — TITLE: Why Is China Giving Away the AI Models It Spent Billions to Build?

Driving question (carries the title): why would Chinese labs pour billions into frontier AI models and
then release the weights for free — handing rivals the very thing OpenAI and Google keep locked?
Three answers, escalating: (1) Popular: marketing, mindshare, recruiting. Incomplete — that doesn't pay
the training bill. (2) Deeper: build a developer ecosystem around Chinese models. Incomplete — explains
adoption, not why give away the crown jewels. (3) Real mechanism: open weights commoditize the layer
America monopolizes; if the model is free, the moat shifts away from the thing the US controls and
toward things China can supply.
LOCK reading: the export controls were meant to keep China dependent on American AI; read backward,
giving the model away is the move that makes the dependency run the other direction.
Honest turn: bullish — erodes the US software-and-compute moat. Bearish — burns capital with no direct
return and may train rivals too. Synthesis: free is leverage only if the ecosystem outlasts the subsidy.
Reframe close: the real question was never "why give it away." It is "what is the moat actually made of
once the model itself is free."
Anchor (verify): Chinese labs open-sourcing frontier model weights (DeepSeek / Qwen lineage) while
Western leaders stay closed; hedge capability and cost claims. NOT the legacy-glut story (separate idea).
Named giant: OpenAI / Nvidia (the moat owners, title-implied). Protagonist: one open-weights release
traced from training cluster to a developer who never paid for it.
