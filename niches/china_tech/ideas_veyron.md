# IDEAS — veyron

Engine fit: every Veyron idea must be RACE-SHAPED — a named Western giant whose lead is naturally
a CLOCK (time-to-the-next-node, decades of accumulated moat, per-chip lead) against a China challenger
that is compressing that clock. The video runs the contest in LEGS, each leg ending on a SPLIT-TIME
(the Western side's time or rate, then China's, in one shared frame), the lead visibly compressing leg
to leg, then ONE honest brake where the lead is a mirage, then a finish-line forecast. If an idea is
about WHY someone did something irrational, it is a Jade idea, not a Veyron idea. If it has no clock,
it is not a Veyron idea.

TITLE RULES: name a Western giant (Intel, ASML, TSMC, Nvidia, Samsung, Micron, SK Hynix) and frame
PACE, not score — a head start, a closing gap, a clock that decides the race. Em dash is fine in the
TITLE only; the SPOKEN script bans em dashes. Machine and code names search near zero on vidiq, so
keep them in the anchor, never the title.

FACT NOTE: anchors below are competitor-sourced (Eastern Engine grid, 2026-06) and operator-supplied,
and dated. The split-times are the spine, so guard them hardest: re-verify every duration and rate at
script time, and if a real time figure is not available, frame the split qualitatively (years against
months, a decade against one product cycle) rather than inventing precise numbers (FACT INTEGRITY +
split-time guard in the story prompt).

## IDEA A — Intel (the node race; rides the hottest fresh wave)

TITLE (draft): Intel Had a Ten-Year Head Start on This Chip — China Closed It Without the One Machine It Needs

Race stake (the clock the whole video runs on): Intel's entire lead is time-to-the-next-node. Can it
stay ahead while a challenger compresses each node cycle WITHOUT extreme ultraviolet lithography?

Anchor (verify at script time; hedge every number): SMIC reportedly reached a seven nanometer class
process and is pushing denser nodes using deep ultraviolet multi-patterning rather than extreme
ultraviolet machines it cannot buy. Eastern Engine's grid framed it as "7nm Beat Intel 18A in one
critical layer, without EUV" and "32.5 versus 36 nanometer". Intel's own roadmap measures node jumps
in multi-year R&D cycles. Treat all node names and the "one critical layer" claim as reported, not
independently benchmarked.

Legs (each closes on a split-time in the shared frame):
1. Design / density — West: a node jump measured in years of R&D. China: comparable density reported
   in fewer cycles via multi-patterning.
2. Manufacturing / yield — West: mature yield of good dies. China: the time to ramp yield (this is
   where the honest brake is seeded).
3. Tooling — doing it without extreme ultraviolet: West, one exposure pass; China, several passes,
   more time and cost per layer.

Honest brake (the lead that is a mirage): the gap that is NOT closing is sub-five-nanometer at volume,
cost per good die, and yield economics. China's clock is fast on a demo wafer and slow on a million.
State the strongest case the lead is real AND the strongest case it is a mirage, then one synthesis line.

Finish-line forecast (close): where node parity actually stands twelve months out, the author's plain
call, and a forward-looking comment-bait question (which node, which year, does the gap cross).

Named giant: Intel (title). Demand: china chip war + Intel-vs-China node fight. Protagonist/object
thread: a single wafer traced through the whole node race, origin to finished die.

## IDEA B — ASML (the lithography clock; the deepest moat)

TITLE (draft): China Can't Buy ASML's Machine — So It's Racing to Make It Pointless

Race stake: ASML's moat is measured in decades of accumulated lead and thousands of suppliers. Can
China's workaround clock — deep ultraviolet multi-patterning, photonic stamping, homegrown lithography
— reach useful VOLUME before ASML's lead compounds further?

Anchor (verify at script time; hedge every number): ASML is the sole maker of extreme ultraviolet
lithography systems, built over roughly two decades with a vast supplier base. China's domestic
lithography is reported to be years behind at the leading edge. Eastern Engine's grid framed adjacent
moves as "Stamped Photonic Chips Without DUV and Cut Costs 90%" and "5nm Without The Machine It Was
Banned From". Treat the cost-cut and node claims as reported demos, not qualified volume production.

Legs (each closes on a split-time):
1. The machine — West: roughly two decades and thousands of suppliers behind extreme ultraviolet.
   China: a homegrown lithography timeline reported years back.
2. The workaround — multi-patterning to fake a smaller node: extra passes and time against a single
   extreme ultraviolet pass.
3. The leapfrog — photonic or imprint stamping that skips deep ultraviolet entirely: the incumbent
   roadmap's time against China's demo time.

Honest brake: demo against volume. The lead is a mirage where throughput and defectivity decide it —
one good chip in a lab is not a million at yield. Hold both the real-lead case and the mirage case.

Finish-line forecast: where the lithography gap stands twelve months out, the author's call, and a
forward-looking question (does the workaround clock ever reach volume, or does the moat compound).

Named giant: ASML (title). Demand: china chip war + the EUV / lithography chokepoint. Protagonist/
object thread: one exposure, a single pulse of patterning light traced from source to silicon.

## IDEA C — Nvidia (the system-scale race; highest demand)

TITLE (draft): Nvidia Wins Every Single Chip — China Is Winning the Clock That Decides AI

Race stake: Nvidia leads per chip and that lead is stable, even widening. But the contest that decides
artificial intelligence is compute per cluster, and China's system-scaling clock is closing. Can
Nvidia's per-chip lead outrun China's rate of scaling weaker chips into one machine?

Anchor (verify at script time; hedge every number): a single domestic accelerator such as Huawei's
Ascend line still trails a single Nvidia chip, by the vendor's own admission. China's answer is system
scale — supernodes wiring thousands of weaker chips into one cluster. Eastern Engine's grid framed the
wave as "No-GPU Supercomputer Beat America By 21%, Officially #1". Treat per-cluster claims as reported,
and keep per-chip (Nvidia clearly ahead) distinct from per-cluster (contested).

Legs (each closes on a split-time):
1. One chip — Nvidia wins cleanly; establish the honesty early. Per-chip lead stable or widening;
   China loses this leg.
2. The cluster — more weaker chips wired as one brain: the time and scale to stand up a frontier
   cluster, where China closes via brute system scale.
3. Self-sufficiency — the rate of domestic accelerator supply, immune to a foreign cutoff.

Honest brake: the clock that will NOT close is frontier-training efficiency, the deep learning software
ecosystem, high bandwidth memory, and energy cost per unit of useful compute. China can match raw
throughput by burning more power and silicon, not by closing the efficiency clock. Hold both cases.

Finish-line forecast: where the compute race stands twelve months out, the author's call, and a
forward-looking question (does scale beat efficiency, and for how long).

Named giant: Nvidia (title). Demand: china chip war + Nvidia-vs-China, highest-demand named giant for
cold start. Protagonist/object thread: one training run traced from a rack of chips to a finished model.

## PRODUCTION NOTE

All three are deliberately race-shaped to stress-test the Veyron engine on its first runs. Idea A is
the priority: it rides the freshest, highest-velocity Eastern Engine wave (the Intel node fight) and is
the cleanest natural clock (time-to-node) for the split-time spine. Idea C is the highest-demand named
giant (Nvidia) and the best demonstration of the honest-brake leg, because the per-chip leg is an
honest loss for China stated up front, which earns trust before the cluster leg closes the gap. Idea B
is the deepest moat and the best test of the "lead that is a mirage" brake against a demo-versus-volume
story. Keep the split-time qualitative wherever a real duration is not verifiable.
